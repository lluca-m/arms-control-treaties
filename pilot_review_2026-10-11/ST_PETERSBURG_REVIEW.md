# St Petersburg (AC-80): export review, 11 October 2026

Reviewed the complete Markdown export, including public properties, both provision rows, all 21 events, all 19 participation rows and the internal log. Checked the operative wording against the local treaty PDF and the ICRC's published customary-law study, Rule 78. This is not a live Notion, attachment-checksum or mobile-layout verification. The Martens protocols and every secondary-source historical detail were not independently rechecked.

## Decision

Keep the layout and depth. The two provision rows correctly separate the use prohibition from the procedural undertaking to invite accessions. Keep the application conditions attached to the prohibition. The original rule and subsequent practice are usefully distinguished.

Approve removing the unrelated 1999 press-briefing attachment from AC-80 on the reported basis that its identical copy is already attached to AC-13. This review has not independently checked those attachments.

## Corrections before closing

1. **Count basis.** Replace the headline “Parties: 19 (as of 11 October 2026)” with “ICRC-listed participants: 19 (17 original signatories and two 1869 accessions; list checked 11 October 2026).” These historical entries do not establish 19 current states parties. The participation notes themselves leave several modern statuses unresolved. Do not use this figure as a verified current-party count in website filters or comparisons. Keep the historical count with its stated basis; leave a specifically current-party count undetermined. The ICRC study explicitly describes these as nineteen states adhering in 1868–69 (Rule 78, p. 272), rather than a present-day state count.

2. **Signature rule.** “Signed” is appropriate for this instrument, whose text supplies a signature date and no signature period. Do not generalize from adoption and signature having the same date: an instrument can open for signature on its adoption date. A generated label must follow the event being recorded. The export still stores this signature under “Opened for Signature”; correct the underlying semantics as well as the public label.

3. **Adoption convention.** The property defines adoption as when wording was finalized/agreed, while the event and coding note say that happened on 16 November and the property gives 11 December. Reconcile the definition. If retaining 11 December as formal adoption of the Declaration, explicitly distinguish it from the Commission's approval of wording on 16 November; do not silently equate the two. Preserve the Julian/Gregorian explanation.

4. **Accession data.** Baden's and Brazil's accession dates appear only in notes in this export; the displayed date columns are blank. Ensure the Participation data and export have a typed accession action and date (11 January and 23 October 1869 respectively). Do not put them in Date signed or invent a separate entry-into-force date. “Acceded” describes an action, not a substitute for a current-status assessment.

5. **Weight threshold.** Retain the strict “less than 400 grammes” language in every generated comparison. The numeric value 400 is a threshold, not a permitted ceiling or a ban inclusive of 400. Replace the categorical sentence “Artillery shells, heavier than 400 grammes, are outside it” with “The text excludes projectiles weighing 400 grammes or more.” Explain the historical artillery distinction separately if useful. The text defines coverage by weight and contents, not by launcher category.

## Historical entities

Use the existing Countries database for historical political entities where needed; no new database is necessary. Keep the instrument's participant label and source in Participation. Do not force links to modern successors.

Do not automatically convert all seven unmatched labels into seven equivalent extinct-country records. Persia needs treatment as a historical name, not an inferred extinction or succession. “Prussia and the North German Confederation” is a composite representation label and should not silently become a new unitary country. “Sweden and Norway” should be explicitly described as the historical union. Preserve those labels without splitting their single source-listed participation into multiple counted parties. An unresolved canonical link may remain blank pending identification; it need not block the substantive page.

For actual historical entities such as Baden, Bavaria, Württemberg and Austria-Hungary, add or reuse historical entries and link them. No inferred succession dates or modern treaty status should follow from those links. The same distinction applies to future USSR/Ottoman records.
