# Page body

## Overview
The Hague Code of Conduct against Ballistic Missile Proliferation is a politically binding set of principles and transparency measures, not a treaty. Subscribing states commit to restraint in developing, testing and deploying ballistic missiles capable of delivering weapons of mass destruction, to not assisting ballistic missile programmes in countries that might be developing or acquiring weapons of mass destruction in contravention of the norms established by, and those countries' obligations under, international disarmament and non-proliferation treaties (Sec. 3(e)), and above all to annual declarations and pre-launch notifications for ballistic missiles and space launch vehicles. It grew out of the Missile Technology Control Regime and was adopted at The Hague on 25 November 2002. It sets no numerical limits and has no verification. 145 states subscribe; China, Pakistan, Iran, Israel and the Democratic People's Republic of Korea are among those that do not.

## Commitments

### Character of the commitments
The Code is politically binding, and participation is "voluntary and open to all States" (Sec. 2(d)). It sets no numerical limits and contains no verification, enforcement, withdrawal or duration clauses. Its restraint commitments are qualified ("maximum possible restraint", "where possible"), so its main new requirement is transparency.

### Principles (Sec. 2)
- The need to prevent and curb the proliferation of ballistic missile systems capable of delivering weapons of mass destruction (2(a)).
- Strengthening and universalising multilateral disarmament and non-proliferation mechanisms (2(b)-(c)).
- Commitment to the 1996 UN Declaration on international cooperation in outer space (2(e)); states should not be excluded from the peaceful benefits of space but must not contribute to missile proliferation (2(f)).
- Space launch vehicle programmes should not be used to conceal ballistic missile programmes (2(g)), and transparency is needed for both (2(h)).

### General measures (Sec. 3)
- Ratify, accede to or otherwise abide by the Outer Space Treaty, the Liability Convention and the Registration Convention (3(a)).
- Curb and prevent the proliferation of WMD-capable ballistic missiles at global and regional level (3(b)).
- Exercise maximum possible restraint in the development, testing and deployment of such missiles and, where possible, reduce national holdings (3(c)).
- Exercise vigilance when assisting foreign space launch vehicle programmes (3(d)).
- Do not contribute to, support or assist ballistic missile programmes in countries that might be developing or acquiring WMD in contravention of their treaty obligations (3(e)).

### Transparency and confidence-building measures (Sec. 4)
- **Ballistic missiles:** an annual declaration outlining ballistic missile policies, and annual information on the number and generic class of missiles launched in the preceding year (4(a)(i)).
- **Expendable space launch vehicles:** an annual declaration of policies and land (test-) launch sites, and annual launch data, consistent with commercial and economic confidentiality; voluntary consideration of inviting international observers to launch sites (4(a)(ii)).
- **Pre-launch notifications** for ballistic missile and space launch vehicle launches and test flights, giving the generic class, launch window, launch area and planned direction (4(a)(iii)).
- Optional bilateral or regional transparency measures (4(b)). These measures do not justify the programmes they cover (4(c)).

## Participation and operation
States do not sign or ratify the Code; they subscribe by sending a note verbale to the Immediate Central Contact in Vienna. The Code took effect on adoption at the launching conference on 25 November 2002 and has no entry-into-force conditions. Austria serves as the Immediate Central Contact (Executive Secretariat); there is no depositary. Declarations and notifications are exchanged through a restricted website and are not public.
- **Meetings and decisions.** Subscribing states meet annually or as agreed, and take all decisions, substantive and procedural, by consensus of those present (Sec. 5(a)-(b)). The meetings define notification procedures, a mechanism for the voluntary resolution of questions on declarations, and the central contact (Sec. 5(c)).
- **Amendment.** The regular meetings may agree amendments to the Code, by consensus (Sec. 5(b)-(c)).
- **Duration and withdrawal.** The Code has no duration clause and sets no procedure for ceasing to subscribe.
- **Membership.** 145 states as of the 25th Regular Meeting (Vienna, 28-29 May 2026, chaired by Jordan). India subscribed in 2016, and the most recent listed subscription is Qatar's (22 January 2024). Other non-subscribers include Egypt, Saudi Arabia, Syria, the United Arab Emirates, Brazil, Indonesia, Algeria, Cuba, Mexico, Malaysia, Thailand and Viet Nam.

## History and compliance

### Origins and negotiation
The Code grew out of the Missile Technology Control Regime. MTCR partners issued a draft International Code of Conduct at their Helsinki plenary in October 2000 and, at the Ottawa plenary in September 2001, agreed to open the text to non-members. At a meeting in Paris in February 2002, more than 80 states agreed on a revised draft with looser language, in which "obligations" became "general measures" ([HCoC chronology](https://www.hcoc.at/what-is-hcoc/hcoc-chronology.html)). Delegations from nearly 100 states, including China, India, Israel and Pakistan, continued negotiations in Madrid on 17-19 June 2002. The Code was adopted at a launching conference hosted by the Netherlands in The Hague on 25 November 2002.

### Institutions and membership
At the first meeting the Netherlands became Chair and Austria the Immediate Central Contact, and the subscribing states renamed the instrument The Hague Code of Conduct. The Netherlands circulated the text as UN doc. A/57/724 (6 February 2003). The Austrian Foreign Ministry reports growth from 93 subscribing states at launch to 145.

### UN General Assembly support
The General Assembly has adopted resolutions supporting the Code at its 59th, 60th, 63rd, 65th, 67th, 69th, 71st, 73rd, 75th and 77th sessions, and resolution 79/29 of 2 December 2024. According to the HCoC chronology, Iran voted against the 2008 First Committee draft, objecting that the Code was negotiated outside the United Nations. According to the UN voting record as compiled by [un-project.org](http://un-project.org/), resolution 79/29 passed by 172 votes to 2 with 10 abstentions, with Iran and the Russian Federation voting against.

### Compliance
The Code has no verification or compliance mechanism, and no formal compliance finding was identified in the sources reviewed. The declarations and notifications are not publicly accessible, which limits public assessment of implementation. In May 2026 the subscribing states expressed concern about the use of ballistic missiles in multiple military conflicts.

### Coding notes
- **Record type and status.** Coded as an instrument because the Code rests on one adopted text with numbered sections; it also has regime features (annual meetings, a rotating Chair, a central contact, a data exchange). "In force" here means the arrangement is operative; a politically binding code has no legal entry into force, so that date is blank.
- **Participating states.** 145 counts subscribing states, not treaty parties, as of 29 May 2026 (press release of the 25th Regular Meeting; the official list, last updated January 2024, also totals 145). Non-subscribers are inferred from absence on that list and are not exhaustive.
- **Subscribers at launch.** The Austrian Foreign Ministry gives 93. The list annexed to A/57/724 contains about 101 states, including some that subscribed on 26 November and 4 December 2002, so the two figures measure different dates.
- **Renaming.** The Netherlands' letter of 11 January 2003 (A/57/724) says the renaming was declared at the subscribing states' first meeting; the HCoC chronology places it in November 2004. The primary document is followed.
- **Oversight and third-party roles.** Oversight is coded "Treaty body or commission" for the Regular Meeting of Subscribing States together with Austria's central contact role; there is no international organization or compliance body. Third-party roles are coded "Yes" only because Sec. 4(a)(ii) contemplates voluntary invitations to international observers at launch sites.
- **Inspection and access.** No inspection or access is required. The field is coded "Voluntary observation" for the same Sec. 4(a)(ii) invitation, so that filters can find it without treating it as compulsory verification.
- **Operating environment.** Coded "Not specific" because the commitments apply to programmes rather than to a particular environment.
- **Undated events.** The Helsinki (October 2000), Ottawa (September 2001) and Paris (February 2002) meetings are dated only to the month in the sources and appear as such in the timeline.
- **2024 vote.** The tally for resolution 79/29 comes from [un-project.org](http://un-project.org/), a secondary compilation; the UN Digital Library record could not be retrieved.

## Research note: HCoC: research notes

- record_type: Judgement call. Coded 'Instrument' because the HCoC rests on a single adopted text (A/57/724) with a fixed adoption date, numbered sections and an instrument_form of 'Code of Conduct'. It also has regime-like features (annual Regular Meetings, a rotating Chair, Austria as Immediate Central Contact, a restricted data exchange); recode as 'Regime' if the database reserves 'Instrument' for documents and 'Regime' for standing arrangements with membership.
- instrument_status: Coded 'In force' to mean the arrangement is operative and has not been terminated; the Austrian Foreign Ministry itself refers to its 'entering into force' in November 2002. Strictly, a politically binding code has no legal entry into force, so entry_into_force is null; 'Not applicable' is the alternative coding.
- number_of_parties: 145 is a count of subscribing states, not parties; the Code is politically binding. Source: press release of the 25th Regular Meeting (29 May 2026) and the official list (last updated January 2024, totals 145). The list page states 'Total: 145 states'; my own count of dated entries also gives 145.
- opened_for_signature: Set to null: the Code is not signed but subscribed to by note verbale, open from adoption on 25 November 2002.
- events.date (partial dates): The Helsinki (October 2000), Ottawa (September 2001) and Paris (February 2002) meetings are dated only to the month in the HCoC chronology, recorded as YYYY-MM.
- historical_background (renaming date): Sources conflict. The Netherlands' letter of 11 January 2003 (A/57/724) says the Subscribing States declared at their first meeting that the Code would 'henceforth be known as The Hague Code of Conduct'; the HCoC chronology attributes the naming decision to a Regular Conference in November 2004 (a date that also seems inconsistent with its own list of meetings). The primary document was followed.
- subscribers at launch: The Austrian Foreign Ministry gives 93 subscribers at launch. The annex to A/57/724 lists 101 states (by OCR count), including some dated 26 November and 4 December 2002, so the two figures were not reconciled.
- events: A/RES/79/29 vote: The UN Digital Library returned HTTP 403. The tally and voting list come from un-project.org, a secondary compilation of UN voting data, and agree with the 172-2-10 tally in search-result summaries of the UN record.
- events: UNGA 67th-77th session resolutions: Not all resolutions are listed as events. The dates of 67/42, 69/44, 71/33, 73/49 and 75/60 appear on hcoc.at; the date of 77/58 was not checked against an official source.
- operating_environment: Coded 'Not specific'. The commitments apply to programmes (ballistic missiles and space launch vehicles), not to a particular environment, although land launch sites and outer space use are mentioned.

## Correction to earlier entry: HCoC: corrections to the earlier entry

- Number of Parties was 143; the official count is 145 subscribing states (official list total; 25th Regular Meeting press release of 29 May 2026). These are subscribing states, not treaty parties.
- 'Opened for Signature' is set to 2002-11-25, but the Code is not open for signature; states subscribe by note verbale to the Immediate Central Contact. This should be null or relabelled as subscription.
- Status 'In Force' needs a qualifier: the Code is politically binding and has no legal entry into force. It is operative since adoption on 25 November 2002.
- Depository, Treaty URL, Key Provisions, Weapons/Systems Limited, Withdrawal/Amendment/Duration clauses and Historical Background were empty. The Code has no depositary; Austria is the Immediate Central Contact (Executive Secretariat).
- Page body abbreviation 'HCOC' should be 'HCoC', the form used by the official website.
