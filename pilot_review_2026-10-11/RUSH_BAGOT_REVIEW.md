## Subject-matter coding

Keep Arms limitation & disarmament. Do not add a demilitarization tag simply because later accounts describe the agreement as demilitarizing the Lakes. Its operative provisions impose naval ceilings, dismantling, construction/arming restrictions and limits on employment; they do not establish a general prohibition of military presence in the area.

However, remove the inference that any permitted armed vessel necessarily rules out demilitarization. A geographically demilitarized regime can have limited exceptions. The codebook should define whether that tag covers partial demilitarization. For this record a defensible note is: “Coded as naval arms limitation: the instrument caps and reduces naval forces in named waters and restricts their employment; it does not establish a general demilitarized zone.”

## Corrections

1. **Original terms versus current operation.** The overview and Covers field state the four-vessel/100-ton/one-cannon limits in the present tense while the later-interpretations section explains substantial departures. Label these “Original 1817 limits” and say the agreement remains in force as subsequently interpreted. Also label information/inspection codes as derived from the original text; the later advance-information requirements are already explained well in the coding note.
2. **Dismantling timing (line 91 and provision 6).** Replace “No deadline” with “No numerical period is specified; dismantling was required forthwith.” That word supplies a timing requirement even without a date or number of days. Keep the absence of an express verification procedure separate.
3. **Termination is not a breach-specific remedy (line 160).** Delete “The only remedy for non-compliance is termination on notice.” The clause does not condition termination on a violation. Use “No enforcement mechanism is specified; either party may terminate on six months’ notice.” This does not purport to exhaust remedies outside the instrument.
4. **1946 date discrepancy.** It appears in the internal log, but not in the public coding notes or event description. Move the qualification into the public event: “Dated by the Canadian text and the US note: 5 December; US Treaties in Force 2025 lists 6 December.” Do not infer why the dates differ. The review did not independently resolve that discrepancy.
5. **March 1865 ordering.** The month-only Adams communication sorts before the 8 March instruction it followed. Display “March 1865, following the 8 March instruction; exact day unknown” and preserve that known sequence without inventing a day. A month-only record need not be assigned the first position in its month.
