# Arms Control Treaties Dataset

An open dataset of arms control treaties coded along multiple dimensions of treaty design. Accompanies Luca, L. (2026), "Strategic Substitutability in Arms Control: Beyond Verification" (Chapter 2 of Luca dissertation, UCLA Department of Political Science).

## Coverage

Over 100 arms control treaties and conventions spanning 1817 to the present, from the Rush–Bagot Agreement through the Treaty on the Prohibition of Nuclear Weapons and ongoing negotiations over lethal autonomous weapons.

## What is coded

Each treaty is coded along multiple dimensions of treaty design, including:

- **Type of limitation**: which stage of the development-to-deployment pipeline the treaty targets — use, development, production, stockpiling, transfer, testing, deployment, behavior
- **Quantitative limits**: zero (complete ban), zero (ban on future production), zero (ban on future use), specific numerical limit, percentage reduction, freeze at current level
- **Verification mechanism**: self-reporting, on-site inspections, satellite monitoring, continuous monitoring, international agency oversight, technical verification, challenge inspections, consultative meetings, declarations
- **Type of weapons**: nuclear, chemical, biological, conventional, small arms, landmines, cluster munitions, autonomous systems, naval, air, fissile material, blinding lasers, and others
- **Strategic substitutability**: whether the major parties possessed alternative capabilities covering the core functions of the constrained weapon at the time of agreement
- **Treaty type**: bilateral versus multilateral
- **Status**: in force, terminated, expired, signed-not-ratified, under negotiation
- **Duration, withdrawal provisions, amendment procedures, dispute resolution mechanisms**
- **Number of parties, signatories, review conferences**
- **Date adopted, date entered into force, expiration date**
- **Key non-signatories**
- **Compliance history**

## Status

This is an **ongoing research project**. The accompanying dissertation chapter (May 2026) draws on a 40-treaty snapshot from this dataset against the framework's two enabling conditions (strategic substitutability and verification). The full dataset is under continued development; coverage and coding will expand over time.

Some fields are unevenly populated across treaties. The README will be updated as fields are completed and the schema is refined.
